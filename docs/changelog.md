| (perf: Lua pool trim) | **The Lua pool gives memory back after a big drop.** `newstate()`'s pool (`class Pool`, `src/wrap/lua.cppm`) carves its free lists from 64 KiB chunks aligned to 64 KiB (a block's chunk is its address with the low 16 bits cleared), each counting its live blocks; the chunks come from `VirtualAlloc` in slabs of 64 KiB doubling to 1 MiB (one OS call per MiB: one per 64 KiB chunk made the stress builds 5 to 10% slower), and a closed state's first chunk is kept for the next state (a state with the standard libraries: 26 us to create and close, as before; 37 us without that). `Pool::trim()` drops the empty chunks' blocks from the free lists in one pass, decommits those chunks and releases slabs left with none. **`lua::trimMemory(state, TrimOptions)`** decides: free bytes of at least 8 MiB, more than the live ones and 8 MiB above their low since the last trim (a fragmented pool isn't walked every frame), at most once a second; **`lua::memoryStats`** (reserved, free, trims) and `lua::getallocf` are new. The Engine calls it at the start of every rendered frame, outside every Lua call, and logs a trim. `_heapmin` after the same drop gave nothing back, so the C heap is left alone. New module `imlui.platform.memory` (`privateBytes()`); new `imlui_bench drop` scenario. **Numbers** (`imlui_bench drop`, `controls` left for an empty page, private MiB after the collection): 5000 rows 47.6 before, 17.7 after the trim; 10 000 rows (80 000 elements, 68 MiB Lua heap at the peak, 92 MiB private) 83.4 before, 24.5 after.
